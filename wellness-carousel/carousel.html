The difference
Chronological age≠Biological age

Your chronological age is simple: the number of years since you were born.

Your biological age is an estimate of how your body is ageing, based on measurable biological signals, including:

  • i.DNA changes
  • ii.Blood biomarkers
  • iii.Inflammation markers
  • iv.Metabolic indicators
Biomarkers of ageing are measurable characteristics that reflect ageing processes and health trajectories.
Moqri et al. (2024), Validation of biomarkers of aging. Nature Medicine.

The science behind it

Your cells
keep a record.

One of the most studied ways to estimate biological age is through

DNA methylation

Chemical changes that influence how genes are expressed. As we age, these patterns change.

Scientists use them to build epigenetic clocks: tools designed to estimate biological ageing.

Introduced one of the most widely recognised epigenetic clock models.
Horvath (2013), DNA methylation age of human tissues and cell types. Genome Biology.

Why does this matter?

Ageing happens
before symptoms appear.

Many changes associated with ageing build gradually over years. Biological markers help researchers better understand:

  • 01Cardiovascular health
  • 02Inflammation
  • 03Metabolism
  • 04Cellular ageing

The goal is not to predict your future perfectly.
It is to understand your health today.

Age-related biological changes contribute to many chronic conditions.
Kennedy et al. (2014), Geroscience: Linking aging to chronic disease. Cell.
